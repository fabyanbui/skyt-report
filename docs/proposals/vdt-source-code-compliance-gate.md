# VDT Initiative Proposal: Source Code Compliance Gate

> **Category:** Software Engineering / DevSecOps / Legal-tech enablement  
> **Status:** Proposed — discovery and pilot required  
> **Owner:** Bùi Đình Bảo  
> **Working name:** SCCG (Source Code Compliance Gate)

## 1. Executive summary

Software teams routinely reuse open-source packages, snippets from public repositories, copied examples, and AI-assisted code to reduce delivery time. The legal right to use, modify, and redistribute each component is not determined by whether the code is publicly accessible. It depends on the applicable license, provenance, attribution and notice requirements, compatibility with the product's distribution model, and the organization's own policies and contracts.

I propose **SCCG**, a lightweight automated control integrated into the pull-request (PR) and CI/CD workflow. It inventories dependencies and license evidence, checks repository and dependency metadata against an organization-defined policy, flags missing provenance or required notices, and routes ambiguous/high-risk findings to a human reviewer before release. The aim is to move source-code compliance checks earlier in development instead of relying only on manual review near delivery.

This is a risk-screening and evidence-generation tool, not an automated legal opinion. Final legal interpretation and exceptions remain with authorized legal, security, and engineering owners.

## 2. Problem statement

### 2.1 The actual legal and operational risks

1. **No license does not mean unrestricted use.** A public repository can be readable while its code remains protected by copyright. If no license grants reuse rights, teams should not assume they may copy, modify, redistribute, or commercially deploy the code.
2. **License obligations may be missed.** Depending on the specific license and distribution/use scenario, obligations can include retaining copyright and license notices, reproducing attribution, providing corresponding source code, documenting modifications, or satisfying patent-related terms.
3. **Dependency risk is transitive.** A project may directly select a permissive package but receive other packages through its dependency tree. Their licenses and notices also need to be considered.
4. **Copied snippets and AI-assisted code lack reliable provenance.** A code fragment may have been copied from a repository, tutorial, forum, or generated by an AI tool without a recorded source, license, or review decision. A scanner may not be able to establish provenance or determine whether similarity is legally problematic on its own.
5. **Ownership and authorization can be unclear.** Code created for an employer/client, under a contract, during assigned work, or using confidential material may be subject to contractual and intellectual-property rules. A permissive open-source license does not override third-party confidentiality obligations or prove that the uploader had authority to publish the code.
6. **Evidence is scattered.** License files, notices, package metadata, approval messages, and release records may live in different places. Teams can struggle to show what was checked, by whom, under which policy version, and why an exception was accepted.

### 2.2 Why this matters to an enterprise such as Viettel

A non-compliant component can trigger rework, release delays, contractual disputes, remediation costs, or restrictions on distributing a product. For enterprise, banking, government, and other regulated-client projects, teams also need a repeatable audit trail and a consistent approval process. The objective is not to ban open source; it is to make reuse traceable and policy-compliant without imposing a heavy manual process on every developer.

## 3. Legal and policy framing

This proposal is based on a practical compliance problem, not a conclusion that any specific file in a repository is infringing.

- **Vietnamese intellectual-property framework:** Vietnam's Law on Intellectual Property and its amendments, together with implementing instruments, govern copyright protection and related rights. Computer programs can be protected works. Whether a particular act of copying, modification, or distribution is permitted depends on the applicable law, license terms, facts, and any relevant contracts.
- **Open-source licenses are conditional permissions:** Licenses such as MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0, LGPL, and GPL have different terms. They should not be treated as interchangeable, and compatibility must be assessed in the context of how components are combined, modified, and distributed.
- **Project and contributor governance:** The OpenSSF Open Source Project Security (OSPS) Baseline recommends clear licensing in a standard repository location and a process for contributors to confirm they are authorized to submit code. These controls are useful engineering practices, not a substitute for local legal review.
- **Policy is organization-specific:** Viettel would need to define allowed, restricted, and prohibited license categories, exception authority, evidence retention, and any additional rules for client or internal repositories. The tool should not hard-code a generic license list as if it were legal advice.

References:
- Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam — legal documents: https://ipvietnam.gov.vn/web/english/legal-documents
- Government of Vietnam legal-document portal — consolidated implementing document on copyright and related rights (published 25 September 2026): https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?classid=0&docid=219630&pageid=27160
- GitHub Docs — Open-source license compliance concepts and PR enforcement: https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/concepts/supply-chain-security/open-source-license-compliance
- OpenSSF — OSPS Baseline, legal controls: https://baseline.openssf.org/

**Important:** This document is an engineering proposal, not legal advice. Legal/Compliance should validate the interpretation of current Vietnamese law and each license before a production policy is adopted.

## 4. Proposed solution: SCCG

### 4.1 Workflow

1. A developer opens or updates a PR.
2. CI collects dependency manifests and lockfiles, scans direct and transitive packages, detects available license identifiers, and checks for repository-level license/notice files.
3. The pipeline generates a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) and a compliance report that records package/version, source, detected license, evidence confidence, policy outcome, and remediation guidance.
4. A policy engine evaluates the report against a centrally maintained policy:
   - **Pass:** evidence and license are within the approved policy.
   - **Warning:** metadata is missing, ambiguous, or needs a human decision.
   - **Block:** a policy-defined prohibited condition is detected, or required approval is absent.
5. A reviewer can submit a documented exception request. An authorized owner approves or rejects it with rationale, scope, expiry/review date, and supporting evidence.
6. The approved report, policy version, scan version, and exception decisions are retained with the PR/release record.

### 4.2 Minimum viable product (MVP)

Keep the first pilot deliberately narrow:

- Run as a CI job on one selected repository and one language/ecosystem.
- Inspect dependency manifests and lockfiles; inventory direct and transitive dependencies where tooling supports it.
- Detect missing or conflicting license metadata and missing repository-level license/notice files.
- Apply a configurable policy with three outcomes: pass, warn, block.
- Publish a readable PR summary and machine-readable JSON report.
- Provide a documented manual-review and exception path.
- Generate an SBOM using an established format/tool rather than inventing a new format.
- Do not automatically reject code based solely on AI-generated-code labels or similarity scores.

### 4.3 Out of scope for the MVP

- Replacing legal counsel or making a definitive infringement finding.
- Guaranteeing that a detected license is correct when package metadata is incomplete or inaccurate.
- Proving the origin of every manually copied or AI-generated line of code.
- Automatically resolving all license compatibility questions, especially those dependent on linking, modification, deployment, or distribution facts.
- Scanning confidential repositories with external services unless the data flow has been approved.

Suggested building blocks for evaluation include existing package ecosystem metadata, SPDX license identifiers, an SBOM tool such as Syft, and a license scanner such as ScanCode Toolkit or an equivalent approved tool. Tool selection should follow a short proof of concept for accuracy, supported languages, offline operation, licensing, and security review.

## 6. Example policy rules

| Finding | Default result | Reason / next step |
|---|---|---|
| Recognized license allowed by approved policy and required notices present | Pass | Record evidence and policy version |
| License identifier missing or multiple conflicting signals | Warn | Request package maintainer evidence or human review |
| License is outside the approved allowlist | Warn or block, according to policy | Legal/Compliance review; do not assume all unfamiliar licenses are prohibited |
| Policy-defined incompatible or prohibited license in a distributable artifact | Block | Replace component or obtain documented approval after legal review |
| Required attribution/notice file missing | Warn or block before release | Generate or add the required notices |
| Dependency has no trustworthy source/provenance metadata | Warn | Verify origin, maintainer, version, and integrity |
| Developer requests an exception | Pending review | Record approver, rationale, scope, and expiry |

These are configurable examples, not a universal legal classification of individual licenses.

## 7. Expected value and measurement

The pilot should establish a baseline before setting targets. Suggested success metrics:

- **Coverage:** percentage of detected direct and transitive dependencies with a recorded license status.
- **Early detection:** number and percentage of actionable findings detected before merge/release.
- **Review efficiency:** median time from finding to disposition; manual review time per PR.
- **Noise:** false-positive and unresolved-warning rates, measured on a labeled sample.
- **Auditability:** percentage of releases with an SBOM, policy version, and review/exception record.
- **Developer friction:** added CI duration and percentage of PRs blocked for actionable reasons.
- **Remediation:** time to resolve high-priority findings and number of repeated violations.

Initial pilot goals should be agreed after measuring the baseline; do not promise a reduction percentage before there is evidence.

## 8. Pilot plan

| Phase | Work | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discover (1 week) | Select a repository, ecosystem, release model, and policy owner; map current manual checks | Baseline and scoped policy draft |
| 2. Prototype (1–2 weeks) | Integrate dependency inventory, license checks, SBOM, and PR report in warning-only mode | Working CI proof of concept |
| 3. Validate (1 week) | Compare results with a human-reviewed sample; assess false positives, coverage, and CI overhead | Validation report and revised rules |
| 4. Controlled enforcement (1 week) | Enable blocking only for a small number of well-defined, high-confidence policy violations; document exception flow | Pilot results and rollout recommendation |

Timing is indicative and depends on repository complexity, access, and legal/policy-owner availability.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

- **Incorrect or missing upstream metadata:** treat scanner output as evidence with confidence, not as a definitive answer; support manual verification.
- **Over-blocking and developer resistance:** begin in warning-only mode, measure noise, then enforce only high-confidence rules.
- **Sensitive source-code exposure:** prefer local/self-hosted scanning for confidential projects and review data flows before enabling SaaS tools.
- **Policy becomes stale:** assign a policy owner, version policy changes, and require review when legal or product distribution conditions change.
- **False confidence from automation:** show uncertainty and escalation paths; require human approval for ambiguous/high-impact cases.
- **Unauthorized source code already present:** include a remediation workflow and preserve evidence; do not silently assume that adding a license later cures earlier unauthorized use.

## 10. Decisions needed from VDT / stakeholders

1. Which repository and technology ecosystem should be used for the pilot?
2. Who owns the allowed/restricted/prohibited license policy and exception approvals?
3. Is the pilot limited to internal development, or does it cover software distributed to clients?
4. Must all scanning run inside Viettel-controlled infrastructure?
5. What evidence-retention and audit requirements apply?

## 11. Recommendation

Approve a time-boxed discovery and warning-only MVP for one representative repository. Measure coverage, accuracy, developer friction, and review time before enabling blocking rules. If the pilot demonstrates value, package SCCG as a reusable CI/CD control that product teams can adopt through a shared pipeline template.

The innovation is the **integration of license/provenance evidence, configurable policy decisions, and auditable exception handling directly into the PR workflow**. It operationalizes a known software-supply-chain governance need; the proposal does not claim that license scanning itself is novel.

## Appendix: questions the tool should help answer

- What third-party components are included in this build, including transitive dependencies?
- What license evidence is available for each component, and how confident is that evidence?
- Which required notices or license files are missing?
- Which findings violate the current organization policy, and which require human interpretation?
- Who approved an exception, for which artifact/version, and until when?
- Can we reproduce the compliance report for a particular release?
